2. Audit Categories

2.1 Technical SEO

Score: 70/100

What Works

  • All 500 crawled pages returned 200 with zero fetch errors and zero mid-crawl redirects; a further 73 sampled sitemap URLs were also 100% 200.
  • Full server-side rendering — raw HTTP GET returns complete body text, no CSR gap for non-JS crawlers.
  • Hreflang is exceptional: 454 pages, 0 missing x-default, 0 malformed codes, self-reference present in 454/454 clusters, and 0 reciprocity failures across 2,267 checked pairs.
  • Sitemap is clean: 1,153 URLs, valid urlset, real lastmod dates, genuinely varied priority, zero noindex/redirecting/non-canonical URLs included.
  • Security headers complete — HSTS with preload, enforcing CSP with object-src 'none' and base-uri 'self', nosniff, strict-origin-when-cross-origin, restrictive Permissions-Policy.
  • robots.txt correct with targeted disallows; HTTP->HTTPS and apex->www are single-hop 301s; nonsense URLs return a hard 404, not a soft one.
  • Average response time 316ms (p90 340ms).
  • Legacy Wix URLs are redirected server-side rather than left to 404.

Findings

Metadata streams into <body> instead of <head> on cold renders under load High

CORRECTED 2026-08-03: the first version of this audit claimed 127 URLs shipped no metadata and lacked generateMetadata. Both claims were wrong — generateMetadata exists and is correct on /{country}/{locale}/legal/[slug] and /doctors/[slug]. The real defect is a streaming race: on a cold, uncached render under concurrent load the HTML shell flushes before generateMetadata resolves, so React streams <title>, rel=canonical and meta description into <body> 80-230KB past </head> (e.g. /portugal/pt/legal/terms-of-service closes head at byte 1,758 and emits <title> at byte 226,198). Load-dependent, not route-dependent: all 127 URLs in one 5-concurrent cache-busted burst reproduced it on 38; the same URLs in small unloaded batches reproduced it on 1; warm ISR hits never show it. It matters because Google ignores rel=canonical placed in <body>, Googlebot crawls in bursts, and stricter consumers (Bing, social scrapers, LLM crawlers) are less forgiving than Google about late metadata. /legal/* is served no-store with no prerendering so every request is a cold render and permanently exposed; doctor profiles are ISR and exposed only on the revalidation miss.

Recommendation: Not "add generateMetadata" — it is already there. Either (1) prerender /legal/* instead of serving it no-store (one route config, removes the permanent exposure, safe because legal content changes rarely), or (2) share one cached fetch between generateMetadata and the page component via React cache() so the metadata promise is already settled when the shell flushes (fixes both segments). Recommend 1 now, 2 as follow-up. Verify with a 5-concurrent cache-busted burst over all 127 URLs asserting title/canonical/description all precede </head>.

The root entry gate, legal pages and global pages are served no-store and unprerendered High

Content pages are correctly ISR-cached (public, max-age=0, s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=300, x-nextjs-prerender: 1). But /, the 7 root-level global pages and the 36 /legal/* pages are all private, no-cache, no-store with no prerendering. The root gate is the URL every brand search and external link hits, and it pays a full origin round-trip every time. CrUX shows TTFB at 2,618ms on phone (Poor) and 1,985ms on desktop in the latest reported week, against a blended 28-day figure of 534ms.

Recommendation: Scope no-store to the authenticated route groups ((admin), (doctor), (auth), /account, /api) and move /, /legal/* and the root marketing pages onto the same ISR treatment the country pages already use. Note these routes also share the missing-metadata and missing-hreflang symptoms — check for one shared layout or route-group config as the upstream cause before fixing them individually.

38.6% of the country-home document is inlined RSC flight payload High

Median served HTML is 230KB; Irish country homes reach 590KB. 230.5KB of /ireland/en's 596.6KB is inlined Next.js RSC flight payload (self.__next_f.push), roughly 91KB of which is the full GP-availability schedule serialised server-side into the document instead of fetched client-side after hydration. The same pattern appears at 55.2% on service pages and 41.5% on doctor profiles. This lines up with /ireland/en being the worst Lighthouse result measured (49, LCP 4,809ms, TBT 1,515ms).

Recommendation: Fetch the availability schedule client-side after hydration rather than serialising it into the RSC payload. Largest single byte win available.

Legacy /post/* Wix URLs blanket-redirect to the blog hub even when the article exists High

/post/diabetes-a-silent-disease 308-redirects to /ireland/en/blog rather than /ireland/en/blog/diabetes-a-silent-disease, which is live at 2,385+ words. Same for /post/getting-a-gp-sick-note-online-simplified and /post/hand-foot-and-mouth-disease. Redirecting a specific article to a listing page is the textbook soft-404 pattern; Google routinely treats it as a 404 and drops the legacy URL's accumulated equity. These Wix URLs still hold SERP positions.

Recommendation: Build a per-article /post/{slug} redirect map to the matching current article, with the hub only as a genuine last-resort fallback. Separately verify /pt/home -> /ireland/pt — on Wix /pt/ was the Portugal market, which would make /portugal/pt the correct target.

79 hreflang targets are absent from the XML sitemap Medium

Mostly locale variants of the /legal hub, e.g. /ireland/pt/legal, /ireland/es/legal. The pages exist and are referenced as alternates but are never submitted in the sitemap.

Recommendation: Either add them to the sitemap or drop them from the hreflang clusters, so the two sources agree.

Trailing-slash URLs take two redirect hops Low

/ireland/ returns 308 to /ireland, which returns 308 to /ireland/en. Every country prefix has this two-hop chain.

Recommendation: Normalise the trailing slash and the locale in a single rule so the chain collapses to one hop.

/favicon.ico returns 404 Low

Browsers and several crawlers request /favicon.ico unconditionally regardless of any <link rel="icon"> declaration.

Recommendation: Add the file or a rewrite at the well-known path.

Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy header absent Low

The only missing header in an otherwise complete security set.

Recommendation: Add Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin.

2.2 Content Quality

Score: 63/100

What Works

  • Blog articles are genuinely YMYL-compliant: 2,385-3,846 words with a visible byline ('Written by Dr Tiago Miguel Figueira, IMC 523449'), a separate clinical reviewer, and reviewedBy/lastReviewed Physician JSON-LD.
  • Service pages median 1,901 words; country homes median 2,172 words; doctor profiles median 1,176 words.
  • Body-copy translation into German, Czech and Romanian reads as human-quality, not machine output.

Findings

English UI strings leak onto 76% of non-English pages Critical

912 of 1,153 sitemap URLs are non-English. Sampling 66, 50 (76%) render hard-coded English UI strings inside otherwise well-translated pages: 'Languages' on 43, 'Pick a time' on 40, 'Specialist care' on 14, 'Book a consultation' on 6, 'Ready when you are' on 6. Reproducible on /ireland/de/services/acute-medical-consultation, /ireland/cs/health/diabetes and /czechia/cs. The surrounding body copy is correctly translated, so this is one shared cross-sell/CTA component that never received the locale — not a translation backlog. It sits directly on the booking CTA.

Recommendation: Wire the shared CTA/cross-sell component to the active locale. Verify by re-running a leak scan across a 60+ URL non-English sample and expecting zero hits.

~90 /health/ condition pages are thin against national health authorities High

290-486 words with 4-5 H2s each and a unique H1 per page, competing on 'diabetes', 'migraine' and 'hypertension' against HSE, NHS and Mayo. They lack the named-clinician byline and reviewer depth the blog articles already have. Examples: /ireland/en/health/diabetes 298w, /ireland/en/health/migraine 290w, /ireland/en/health/hypertension 323w.

Recommendation: Pick one path deliberately. Either deepen to 1,200+ words with the same byline/reviewer/lastReviewed treatment the blog uses, or reposition as service-intent pages ('Diabetes care in Ireland — book an online GP') and stop competing with national health bodies on informational intent. The current middle ground is the worst outcome for a YMYL site.

The global /blog hub renders 'No articles published yet' Critical

/blog is 232 words and shows no articles, while 44 country-scoped article URLs serve full 2,400-3,800 word content. It sits in the main navigation of every page, in the sitemap, and is the URL llms.txt points AI crawlers at.

Recommendation: Fix the locale/country filter that excludes everything at the unscoped route.

Cross-country service pages are near-duplicates Medium

Sampling the mental-health service across ES/PT/BR found an identical section template with near-verbatim sub-headers and bullet lists, with the country name and local details swapped.

Recommendation: Add genuine per-market substance: local regulator, local pricing, local referral pathways, local clinician names.

Two competing sets of legal pages, both indexable Medium

/terms (687 words) and /privacy (547 words) at the root coexist with /{country}/{locale}/legal/terms-of-service (6,414-8,207 words) and /legal/privacy-policy (3,496-4,358 words). This is both a duplication problem and a compliance ambiguity about which document governs.

Recommendation: Decide which set is canonical, then redirect or noindex the other.

Per-country blog index pages are thin Low

230-430 words each. Less critical than the /health/ pages since these are index pages by design.

Recommendation: Add an intro paragraph and topic groupings per country.

2.3 On-Page SEO

Score: 62/100

What Works

  • Zero pages without an <h1> across all 500 crawled — a perfect result.
  • Of the 461 meta descriptions present, 100% sit in the 70-160 character band. Zero too short, zero too long.
  • Zero titles under 30 characters; keyword-bearing content is consistently placed at the front of the title.
  • Internal linking within each country silo is strong — median 53 links per page, max 128, only one page under 20.

Findings

46% of titles exceed 60 characters and truncate in the SERP High

232 of 500 titles exceed 60 characters; 99 exceed 70. The template appends both a qualifier and the brand, so the truncated tail is exactly the trust signal that earns the click ('IMC-Registered', 'Colegiados', 'CLK Registered'). 32 of 33 country-home pages — the highest-value commercial pages on the site — are over-length. Worst case is 91 characters. Two separators (| and ·) are also mixed inconsistently across page types.

Recommendation: Drop the '| Global Health' / '· Global Health' suffix when the title already exceeds ~48 characters — Google appends the site name from WebSite schema, which is present on all 500 pages. Shorten the middle qualifier. Standardise on one separator. Target 60 characters / ~575px.

Blog article titles are set in ALL CAPS Medium

e.g. 'HAND, FOOT AND MOUTH DISEASE: SIGNS AND TREATMENT · Global Health'. Google frequently rewrites all-caps titles, and they read as low-quality in a YMYL SERP.

Recommendation: Convert blog article titles to sentence case.

Wrong title mapped to a Romanian service page High

/romania/en/services/controlul-greutatii — slug means 'weight control' — carries the title 'Men's Health Online | Romania | Confidential English Doctor'. This is a genuine content bug, not just a duplicate title.

Recommendation: Fix the title and audit neighbouring Romanian service slugs for the same mis-mapping.

55 pages share 12 meta descriptions Medium

All per-country blog hubs sharing one description per language — e.g. 'Evidence-based guides written and reviewed by our medical team. No ads, no fluff.' on 7 pages, with Spanish, Portuguese, Czech and Romanian equivalents on 5-6 each.

Recommendation: Insert the country name into the template. One-line change that makes six SERP listings distinct instead of identical.

Cross-country blog syndication produces identical titles Low

/ireland/en/blog/hand-foot-and-mouth-disease-signs-and-treatment and /portugal/en/blog/hand-foot-and-mouth-disease-signs-and-treatment carry the same title with no country differentiation. Same for /portugal/en/see-a-specialist and one other page.

Recommendation: Add the country to syndicated article titles.

Five blog articles emit two <h1> elements Low

Page title and article title both rendering as h1 in the blog article template. Google handles multiple H1s, so severity is low.

Recommendation: Demote one to h2 in the blog article template.

The root entry gate is the weakest page on the site structurally High

114 words, 6 internal links, one h1, no hreflang, no BreadcrumbList. Every external link, brand search and all crawl equity lands here and distributes through exactly six anchors. There is also no cross-silo linking, so authority earned by an Irish article cannot reach its Spanish equivalent. Gating by country is legitimate for a multi-jurisdiction medical service, but the gate does not need to be thin, hreflang-less and breadcrumb-less to do that job.

Recommendation: Add hreflang and a BreadcrumbList to /. Add trust signals and a short description of the service with links to /about and the doctor hubs, without breaking country selection. Add a visible 'also available in' row site-wide to create cross-silo links — the hreflang clusters already know the mapping.

2.4 Schema / Structured Data

Score: 82/100

What Works

  • Zero JSON-LD parse errors across all 500 pages.
  • The site-wide MedicalOrganization + WebSite block is on all 500 pages and well built — real sameAs covering Wikidata, LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok plus 12+ regulatory bodies (Medical Council, HSE, HIQA, HPRA, RCPI, ICGP), ImageObject logo with dimensions, PropertyValue registrations, correct @id cross-linking.
  • 176 Physician blocks carry solid credential and regulator linkage.
  • Article markup has all required fields.
  • Broad type coverage: BreadcrumbList 325, FAQPage 293, Organization 255, ReserveAction 196, MedicalProcedure 172, MedicalClinic 144, Service 52, Article 50, ItemList 39, Offer 34.

Findings

Product/Brand schema misused on 6 pricing pages High

Care-plan subscriptions on /ireland/*/pricing are typed as Product and are missing the required itemCondition, so they are not eligible for Merchant listings even as currently typed.

Recommendation: Switch to Service + Offer — the pattern already used correctly in 52 other places on the site. Corrected JSON-LD is in findings/schema.md.

No AggregateRating or Review markup anywhere on the site High

Zero rating or review structured data across 500 pages, despite live Doctify review widgets running on the site. Star ratings in the SERP are one of the largest CTR levers available to a medical service.

Recommendation: Add AggregateRating/Review markup reflecting genuinely collected Doctify reviews.

MedicalBusiness vs MedicalClinic type conflict on the same entity Medium

'Global Health · Ireland' is typed as MedicalClinic on the country home and MedicalBusiness on service pages, with neither carrying address, geo or @id. Both are LocalBusiness subtypes, which give a virtual-only provider no local-business eligibility anyway.

Recommendation: Collapse to MedicalOrganization with a country-scoped @id.

BreadcrumbList generator gap on localised service pages Medium

175 of 500 pages lack BreadcrumbList. Most are legitimate (legal and utility pages), but non-English and non-Ireland localised service pages are missing it while their Irish English equivalents have it.

Recommendation: Extend the breadcrumb generator to all localised service routes.

Missing high-value schema for a telemedicine site Medium

No WebSite.potentialAction (SearchAction), no MedicalCondition entity typing on blog 'about', per-country pricing schema exists only for Ireland, and lab-test detail pages have no per-test MedicalTest or Offer.

Recommendation: Add each; they map directly to the site's existing content.

Minor property corrections Low

Article.image should be an ImageObject with explicit width and height. Physician should carry a structured medicalSpecialty rather than free text. MedicalClinic.reviewedBy is not a valid schema.org property on that type — Google ignores it silently, so no benefit is being gained from it.

Recommendation: Correct all three.

293 FAQPage blocks no longer earn a SERP feature Info

Google retired FAQ rich results for non-authoritative sites. The content is well-scoped and contextual, so there is no reason to remove it — just no reason to expand it either.

Recommendation: No action. Do not invest further in FAQ markup expansion.

/ireland/en scores 49 in Lighthouse with LCP 4,809ms and TBT 1,515ms High

The worst page measured, against 91/2,988ms/87ms on a doctor profile, 78 on the entry gate and 76 on a service page. Root cause identified: 230.5KB of the 596.6KB document is inlined RSC flight payload, ~91KB of which is the full GP-availability schedule serialised server-side.

The LCP element, LCP subparts and render-blocking-resources audits all returned empty from Lighthouse 13.4.1 — a known insight-audit migration gap in that version. Hero and logo images are the inferred candidates but this is unconfirmed.

2.6 AI Search Readiness (GEO)

Score: 74/100

What Works

  • AI crawler access is fully open and explicit — robots.txt names GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, Google-Extended, Gemini-Deep-Research, PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User, all permissive, with a permissive wildcard covering the rest.
  • llms.txt is present, returns 200, is well-formed, and lists 30+ country and service links with direct-answer descriptions.
  • Every page is fully server-rendered, so there is no JS gap for AI crawlers.
  • Strong authority signals via the MedicalOrganization sameAs block covering Wikidata, major social profiles and 12+ regulatory bodies.
  • Blog articles carry Article + Physician schema, named physician author with IMC credential and memberOf, a separate clinical reviewer byline, and datePublished/dateModified — exactly the E-E-A-T pattern YMYL content needs.

Findings

llms.txt points AI crawlers at the broken /blog hub and omits individual articles High

The site's strongest and most citable content — the 44 full-length blog articles — is undiscoverable from the one file specifically intended to advertise it, because llms.txt links only the hub, and the hub renders 'No articles published yet'.

Recommendation: Fix the /blog hub, list individual articles in llms.txt, and add an ## Optional section.

Clinician bylines and reviewer schema exist only on blog articles Medium

Service pages — where commercial queries land and where YMYL trust matters most — have no named author or medical reviewer attribution.

Recommendation: Extend the byline/reviewer/lastReviewed pattern from blog articles to service pages.

Hub and /about H2s are marketing copy rather than question-form headings Medium

Question-shaped H2s are one of the strongest passage-extraction signals for AI answer engines.

Recommendation: Rewrite hub and /about H2s into question form.

Long article paragraphs are not chunked for passage extraction Medium

Articles run 2,400-3,800 words in long paragraph blocks rather than self-contained answer units.

Recommendation: Chunk into self-contained ~140-160 word blocks that answer one question each.

No Wikipedia entity Low

Expected for a brand founded in 2023. Wikidata is already present in sameAs.

Recommendation: Backlog — pursue once independent coverage supports notability.
