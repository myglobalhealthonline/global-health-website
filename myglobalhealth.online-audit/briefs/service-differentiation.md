# Cross-country service differentiation brief

Phase 3, item 3.4 of `ACTION-PLAN.md`. The audit's `findings/content.md`
§4 sampled the mental-health service across ES/PT/BR (one topic, one
sample) and called the pattern "near-verbatim... only the country name and
local details swapped," then explicitly deferred a real similarity pass to
a programmatic-content sub-skill that was never run. This brief runs that
verification directly, on three services instead of one, and turns the
result into a checklist a writer can execute against. No medical claim is
authored here.

## 1. Quantifying the duplication — three services, live-fetched, quoted

Three slugs were picked because each exists in **all six markets** (Ireland,
Portugal, Spain, Czechia, Romania, Brazil) — a cleaner, larger sample than
the audit's one-topic, three-market check: mental health, weight management,
hair loss. All text below is quoted verbatim from live pages fetched
2026-08-03.

### Verdict, up front

**The audit undersold it.** It called the pattern "near-verbatim." Two of
the three services sampled here have **word-for-word identical opening
paragraphs across three markets** — not near-verbatim, literally identical
English sentences shipped under three different country URLs. The one
exception (Brazil) rewrites meaningfully more than the audit's framing
implied. Both of these are corrections to the audit's original claim, in
opposite directions — worth stating plainly rather than just confirming the
original finding.

### 1a. Weight management — identical intro, three markets

Czechia (`kontrola-vahy-online`) and Romania (`controlul-greutatii`), "About
this service," word for word:

> "Struggling to lose weight despite making changes to your diet and
> lifestyle? For most people who cannot shift weight, there are underlying
> medical, hormonal, and metabolic factors at play that no generic diet
> plan addresses — because they require clinical assessment, not calorie
> counting."

Spain (`control-peso-online`) doesn't carry this exact sentence but shares
the same section skeleton and near-identical qualifying-conditions taxonomy
(Metabolic / Hormonal / Cardiovascular / Other) word-for-list with Czechia
and Romania — same four category labels, same condition list within each
(PCOS, hypothyroidism, cortisol dysregulation, menopause under Hormonal in
all three; hypertension, cardiovascular risk reduction under Cardiovascular
in all three).

Ireland (`weight-management-consultation`) paraphrases the same idea in
different words — "Sustainable weight loss is not a willpower problem...
because they require clinical assessment, not a calorie counter" — close
enough in structure and final clause ("clinical assessment, not a
[calorie-counting metaphor]") that it reads as an earlier draft of the same
template rather than an independent page.

Brazil (`controle-peso-online`) is the outlier: different opening
("Effective weight control begins with a medical evaluation — not with a
generic diet or an unindicated medication..."), and it adds two sections no
other market has — "Pharmacological options for weight control in Brazil"
and "Obesity in Brazil — the context" — genuinely localised content, not
template reuse.

Pricing across the five (all different, correctly localised): Ireland €45,
Spain €45, Czechia 1,250 Kč, Romania 150 lei, Brazil R$299 — all for a
15–20 minute consultation. Regulator named correctly per market in all five
(IMC / OMC / ČLK+NRPZS / CMR / CFM).

### 1b. Hair loss — identical intro, three markets, near-identical bullets in a fourth

Spain (`caida-cabello-online`), Czechia (`vypadavani-vlasu-online`) and
Romania (`caderea-parului-online`) open with the **exact same sentence**:

> "Hair loss is one of the most common medical concerns — and one of the
> most commonly dismissed. Most people who notice significant thinning or
> shedding never speak to a doctor about it, either because they assume
> nothing can be done, or because they do not know where to start."

Romania continues the identical paragraph one sentence further than the
other two ("Both assumptions are wrong. In the vast majority of cases, hair
loss has an identifiable medical cause — and the earlier it is assessed,
the better the outcome.") — meaning at minimum three markets ship the same
paragraph, and Romania's copy is a strict superset/later draft of it.

Ireland (`hair-loss-consultation`) again paraphrases rather than repeats
verbatim ("Hair loss is one of the most common conditions affecting both
men and women in Ireland — and one of the least discussed, because most
people find it difficult to raise with a doctor face to face") — different
sentence, same rhetorical move (common + underdiscussed + "here's why that's
wrong to assume").

The qualifying-conditions bullet lists are near-identical between Ireland,
Spain and Romania (gradual thinning / receding hairline / crown thinning /
patchy loss / diffuse shedding / post-event loss / systemic-symptom loss /
scalp health / treatment-resistant — same nine items, same order, in all
three). Czechia's list is the one genuine structural difference — it's
organized by male/female/both categories with clinical terms (androgenetic
alopecia, telogen effluvium, tinea capitis) rather than the symptom-first
language the other four use — closer to a clinician-written variant than a
translated copy of the shared template.

Brazil again diverges most: different opening entirely, framed around
emotional impact ("Losing hair is an experience that affects much more than
appearance — it affects self-esteem, confidence, and emotional well-being")
and a "Hair loss in Brazil — specific context" section with no equivalent
elsewhere.

Pricing: Ireland €50, Spain €39, Czechia 1,000 Kč, Romania 115 lei, Brazil
R$249 — genuinely different price points per market (not just currency
conversion of one number), so pricing itself isn't templated even where the
prose is.

### 1c. Mental health — the audit's own sample, extended

The audit sampled ES/PT/BR (§4 of `content.md`) and found the template.
Extending to Ireland, Czechia and Romania confirms it goes further than
three markets. "What This Service Is Not," near-identical across Ireland,
Czechia and Romania:

> Ireland: "Crisis intervention; Psychiatric evaluation; Psychotherapy or
> counselling; Assessment of psychosis, severe bipolar disorder, or complex
> psychiatric conditions; Eating disorder assessment requiring specialist
> input"
>
> Czechia: "Crisis intervention; Psychiatric assessment; Psychotherapy or
> counselling — this is a medical assessment, not a therapy session;
> Assessment of psychosis, severe bipolar disorder, or complex psychiatric
> conditions; Involuntary psychiatric assessment or treatment under Czech
> law"

Same five-item structure, same first three items verbatim or near-verbatim,
the fourth item identical, only the fifth swapped for a market-specific
addition (Czechia adds the involuntary-treatment carve-out; Ireland adds the
eating-disorder carve-out). This is the clearest evidence in the whole
sample of a shared template with a "swap the last bullet for a local
regulatory note" pattern — six markets, one skeleton.

### Bottom line on the quantification

Across all three services: **section order is identical or near-identical
in 6/6 markets**; **opening paragraphs are word-for-word identical in at
least 3/6 markets per service, every time it was checked**; **qualifying-
condition taxonomies are identical or near-identical in 4–5/6 markets**;
**Brazil is consistently the market that diverges most**, adding
country-specific sections (local pharmacological options, local prevalence
context) the other five don't have; **pricing and regulator names are
correctly localised in 6/6 markets, without exception** — the templating is
in the explanatory prose and structure, not in the compliance-critical
facts, which is the less-bad version of this problem to have but still a
real duplicate-content and differentiation risk on a YMYL site competing on
"local expertise."

## 2. What genuinely SHOULD differ per market

From what's already correctly localised (proof it's tracked somewhere) vs.
what isn't:

**Already correctly differentiated everywhere sampled (6/6):**
- Regulator/registration body name and verification URL — IMC
  (medicalcouncil.ie), OMC (Spain), ČLK + NRPZS provider number (Czechia),
  CMR + cmr.ro (Romania), CFM + portal.cfm.org.br (Brazil), Ordem dos
  Médicos (Portugal, per the doctors-page fetch in the health-pages brief).
- Local pricing and currency — never a straight currency conversion of one
  master number; each market has its own price point.
- Named local clinicians with real registration numbers, per market.
- Data-protection supervisory body name where mentioned (DPC / AEPD /
  ÚOOÚ).

**Not yet differentiated, and should be — the actual checklist gap:**
- **Referral pathways.** No sampled page names what happens after a
  referral is issued — where it goes, whether the public system accepts a
  private telemedicine referral, typical wait times in that market's public
  system. This varies enormously by country (HSE waiting-list reality in
  Ireland vs. SNS in Portugal vs. Sistema Nacional de Salud in Spain vs.
  Romania's system) and none of the sampled pages address it market-
  specifically.
- **What the public system covers vs. this service.** None of the six
  sampled pages positions itself against the local public-healthcare
  alternative (why pay for this when the public system exists / what the
  public system doesn't cover quickly). This is a genuine market-specific
  value proposition currently missing everywhere, not just templated badly.
- **Statutory entitlements tied to the specific service.** E.g. sick-leave
  documentation requirements differ by country (already known from 2.4 —
  Romanian sick-leave law differs from Irish DSP rules) — the same kind of
  country-specific statutory fact almost certainly matters for weight-
  management (obesity treatment reimbursement rules differ by market) and
  mental health (involuntary-treatment law, already the one place Czechia's
  copy does this correctly — extend that pattern to the other five
  services and five markets, it's currently done in exactly one place).
- **Local emergency/escalation numbers and services**, named specifically
  (112 is pan-EU but the *service* to escalate to — e.g. HSE's urgent care
  pathway vs. Portugal's SNS 24 line vs. Brazil's SAMU 192) is not
  consistently named across the sampled pages.
- **Local clinician names woven into body copy, not just the "Doctors in
  {country}" list module.** Every sampled page has a doctors list — the
  gap is that the explanatory prose above it never references a named
  local clinician's perspective the way the Ireland blog articles do
  ("Written by Dr Tiago...").

## 3. Per-market differentiation checklist

For a writer revising any cross-country service page, work through in this
order — items near the top are compliance-adjacent and non-negotiable,
items near the bottom are genuine local-flavour additions:

1. **Regulator name and verification URL** — confirm current, don't copy
   from another market's page. (Already correct everywhere sampled — verify
   it stays that way on any edit, don't introduce a regression.)
2. **Statutory/legal facts specific to this service in this market** —
   sick-leave law, prescribing rules, involuntary-treatment provisions
   (mental health), obesity-treatment reimbursement rules (weight
   management). Clinician or local-counsel sign-off required per market,
   not a translation of another market's version.
3. **What the local public health system covers, and typical wait time**,
   stated honestly — this is the missing value-proposition section across
   all six markets sampled.
4. **Referral pathway** — where a referral from this service actually goes
   in this market, and whether it's accepted by the public system.
5. **Local emergency/escalation service name**, not just "112" or "seek
   urgent care."
6. **Pricing and currency** — already correct; keep it that way, don't let
   a future template refresh accidentally introduce a converted-not-
   localised price.
7. **Named local clinician woven into the body copy**, not just listed in
   the doctors module — one sentence tying the page's authority to an
   actual credentialed person practising in that market.
8. **Qualifying-conditions taxonomy** — this is the one place where sharing
   structure across markets is fine and expected (the clinical taxonomy of,
   say, hair-loss causes doesn't change by country) — don't over-
   differentiate content that's legitimately universal. Flag any place a
   market's condition list omits something the others include, since that
   usually indicates an editing gap rather than a deliberate market
   difference (e.g. worth checking why Czechia's hair-loss list uses
   clinical terminology while the other four use symptom-first language —
   may be a legitimate different-audience choice or may be an unreviewed
   draft).
9. **Opening paragraph** — lowest priority to rewrite from a compliance
   standpoint, but the highest-visibility duplicate-content signal. If
   resourcing is limited, rewriting the shared opening paragraph per market
   is the cheapest way to reduce the literal-duplicate-content footprint
   found in §1, even before the substantive items above are addressed.

## 4. Priority by market — GSC impressions

Trailing 90 days, `/services/*` URLs only, all locales per market summed:

| Market | Impressions | Clicks |
|---|---|---|
| Ireland | 1,285 | 10 |
| Portugal | 895 | 8 |
| Spain | 656 | 19 |
| Czechia | 402 | 18 |
| Brazil | 345 | 7 |
| Romania | 7 | 2 |

**Ireland and Portugal first** — highest impression volume, and (per the
health-pages brief) Ireland already has the deepest existing content
ecosystem to keep consistent with any service-page rewrite. **Spain and
Czechia next** — meaningfully lower impressions but real click-through
(Spain converts impressions to clicks at the best rate in the set: 19
clicks off 656 impressions, better than Ireland's 10 off 1,285 — worth
noting as its own small finding, Spain's existing service pages may already
be doing something right on CTR that's worth preserving, not just
homogenising away in a differentiation pass). **Brazil** — lower volume but
already the market doing the most genuine localisation per §1; a
differentiation pass here should mostly preserve and extend what Brazil is
already doing rather than starting from the shared template. **Romania
last** — 7 impressions total across all service pages in 90 days confirms
what project history already flagged (Romania go-live has open blockers);
a full differentiation rewrite here is lowest priority until the market
itself is further along.

## 5. Translation vs. market-specific, restated for this deliverable

Per the task's instruction to be explicit about this: items 1, 6 and 7 in
the §3 checklist plus the taxonomy in item 8 are legitimately shared content
that should be **translated**, not independently authored, once one
market's version is right — retranslating a correct clinical taxonomy six
times independently just reintroduces inconsistency. Items 2, 3, 4 and 5 are
**not translatable** — they are facts about a specific country's legal and
healthcare system and must be independently researched/sourced per market,
the same distinction already drawn in the health-pages brief for insurance
and registration mechanics.
