Only about one pregnancy in twenty ends on its estimated date.02How the due date is calculatedThe estimate is Naegele's rule: 280 days, or 40 weeks, from the first day of your last period. That assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14.If your cycles are longer or shorter, ovulation moves with them, so the calculator shifts the date by the difference. A 32-day cycle puts the estimate four days later; a 24-day cycle brings it four days earlier.Worked example: a last period starting on 1 January with a 28-day cycle gives 8 October. The same period with a 32-day cycle gives 12 October.03Why a dating scan replaces this estimateA calculator can only work with the dates you give it. A scan measures the baby, which is why its date is the one your maternity team keeps using afterwards.It depends on remembering the first day of your last period accurately.It assumes you ovulated in the middle of your cycle, which not everyone does.Irregular cycles, recently stopped contraception and breastfeeding all make the last period a weak anchor.A first-trimester scan measures the baby, usually between about 11 and 14 weeks, and is accurate to within a few days.After IVF the dating comes from the transfer date rather than a period, so this estimate does not apply.04When to contact a doctor or midwifeYou think you are pregnant and have not yet arranged antenatal care — the first appointment and the dating scan both belong early.You have bleeding, cramping or pain on one side in early pregnancy.You have severe or constant vomiting, or cannot keep fluids down.You have a long-term condition or take regular medicines — some need reviewing as soon as a pregnancy is confirmed.You have reached 41 weeks, or you are past your due date and the baby's movements have changed.Where to go from hereAn estimated date is where antenatal care starts, not the whole of it. Only about one pregnancy in twenty ends on its estimated date, and the great majority of births happen in the weeks either side of it. A first-trimester scan is the accurate version, and once you have had one, its date is the one that is used.How many weeks pregnant am I?Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last period, not from conception — which is why you are already around four weeks pregnant when a test first turns positive. Conception happens roughly two weeks after that first day, so the pregnancy is always dated about two weeks 'older' than the embryo.What does 9w 3d mean?Nine completed weeks and three days since the first day of your last period. Maternity records are written this way because a whole week is too coarse when a scan or a test has to happen inside a particular window.Can I use this if my cycles are irregular?You can, and entering your usual cycle length will get you closer than the 28-day default. But an irregular cycle is exactly the case where the last period is a weak anchor, so treat the result as a rough guide until you have been scanned.Does the due date change after a scan?Often, yes — usually by a few days. The scan measures the baby instead of counting from a remembered date, so it is the one that is kept. A change of a week or more is common when the date of the last period was uncertain.Does this due date calculator store my details?No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser.
