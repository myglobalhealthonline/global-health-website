In Portugal it is provided through the SNS by the International Vaccination Centres (CVI) and public health units, and privately as well. Part of it — risk assessment, reviewing your vaccination record, malaria prophylaxis, medicines to carry, managing chronic illness while travelling — can be done by video. Administering vaccines and issuing the international certificate of vaccination cannot: those are in-person acts, and the yellow fever vaccine in particular is given only at an International Vaccination Centre.Risk assessed by destinationVaccines in person, at a CVIYellow fever: CVI onlyBook a travel health consultationTravel consultation on SNS 24What this guide coversWhat a travel consultation actually assesses, and why the destination changes everything.Where it is done — CVI, public health units, private — and which documents to bring.The line between what a video consultation settles and what requires travelling to a centre.Recommended lead time, the vaccines each country requires and waiting times all change by destination and season. They are not quoted here: each points at SNS 24, the CVI, the IHMT or the WHO.GHGlobal Health Medical TeamReviewed by the Global Health Medical Team.Starting pointWhat a travel consultation actually isIt is not a consultation about vaccines. It is a consultation about your trip, in which vaccines are only one of the possible outputs.SNS 24 describes it as the consultation in which the traveller is informed about the preventive (or curative) measures to adopt before, during and after the journey, according to the destination. The important part is the last clause: according to the destination. The same person, travelling to Amsterdam or to rural Mozambique, leaves with recommendations that look nothing like each other.What changes the assessment is not only the country. It is the region within it, the season, the type of accommodation, the length of stay, whether you are going rural or urban, whether you are visiting relatives, whether you are taking children, whether you are pregnant, whether you have a chronic illness or are immunosuppressed, and whether you plan any high-risk activity. Two people holding the same plane ticket can have very different assessments.Destination and itinerary — country, region, rural or urban, altitude.Season — rainy season, transmission periods, active outbreaks.Type of trip — tourism, work, backpacking, visiting family, humanitarian mission.Your health — pregnancy, age, chronic illness, immunosuppression, regular medication.Vaccination history — what you already have, what has lapsed, what is missing.Official description: SNS 24 — consulta do viajante. For complex itineraries or higher-risk destinations the IHMT is Portugal's reference centre for tropical medicine, and the fullest country-by-country guidance in English is on TravelHealthPro.LocationsWhere it is done and what to bringThere are three possible routes, and they are not interchangeable for everything.Within the SNS, travel consultations and international vaccination are carried out at International Vaccination Centres and at the public health units of the local health authorities, by prior appointment. Each region publishes its own locations and opening hours. Privately, the consultation is offered by hospitals and clinics, and by telemedicine services. These are different circuits with different competences — the next section sets out exactly where the line falls.International Vaccination Centre — the consultation and, above all, international vaccination and the certificate.Public health unit / health centre — advice and vaccination that is not CVI-exclusive, depending on the region.Private consultation, in person or by video — risk assessment, prescription and preparation; vaccination still requires attending in person.SNS 24 lists the documents to bring: identification, a document showing your número de utente and your vaccination record. Two more are worth adding: the list of your regular medication, and the itinerary with dates. Without the itinerary the consultation loses half its value, because the risk assessment depends on exactly that.Book before everything else is arrangedIdeally book as soon as the trip is decided, not in the final week. Some vaccines need time to produce protection and some require more than one dose. Appointments at International Vaccination Centres can also carry a waiting list — check availability in your region rather than assuming a date.Locations and hours by region: ARS Norte — CVI · ARS Algarve — traveller health · SNS — healthcare abroad.PreventionVaccines, yellow fever and the international certificateOne distinction settles most of the confusion: vaccines that are recommended, vaccines that are required, and vaccines only a CVI can give.Recommended vaccines protect against risks present at the destination. Required vaccines are those a country imposes as a condition of entry, proven by the international certificate of vaccination. Yellow fever is the best-known case of both at once: health authorities determine that it is administered at International Vaccination Centres, and that is where the certificate is issued.Reviewing the national vaccination schedule is part of the consultation: many trips reveal only that routine vaccines are overdue.Some travel vaccines require more than one dose, with an interval between them.Entry requirements change and are set by each country — confirm with the destination's authority and the WHO, not in an article.The international certificate of vaccination is an official document issued at the moment of vaccination in a CVI. Nobody issues it remotely.Vaccination does not replace the non-vaccine measures, which matter more at a good number of destinations: protection against mosquito bites, care with water and food, prevention of traveller's diarrhoea, safe sexual behaviour, sun exposure, and road safety — which is, in practice, one of the most common causes of serious harm while travelling.On yellow fever vaccination at CVIs: ARS Algarve. Country requirements: WHO — travel advice.TransparencyWhat a video consultation can — and cannot — doWe put this early and plainly, because almost no telemedicine service writes it down.A video consultation does not administer vaccines and does not issue the international certificate of vaccination. If your trip requires yellow fever, you will have to attend an International Vaccination Centre, regardless of who performs the prior assessment. Any service suggesting otherwise is promising what it cannot deliver.That said, the part of a travel consultation that is not an injection is considerable — and it is precisely the part people tend not to make time for:Risk assessment of your actual itinerary, with your clinical history in front of the doctor.Review of your vaccination record and identification of what is missing before you book the CVI — so you go once rather than twice.Malaria prophylaxis where indicated: choice of drug, regimen and effects to watch for.Medicines to carry: traveller's diarrhoea, nausea, pain, allergy, and what to do if you fall ill far from home.Chronic illness while travelling: adjusting timing across time zones, storing medication, quantities, and a medication letter for your luggage.After the trip: which symptoms require medical assessment on your return, and within what timeframe.You can confirm any doctor's registration with the Ordem dos Médicos, with us as anywhere else.No consultation guarantees a vaccine or a certificatePrescribing prophylaxis depends on the clinical assessment and your history. Vaccination and the international certificate depend on the in-person international vaccination circuit. A serious consultation tells you which case is yours; it does not promise a document before examining you.After the tripOn your return: the symptoms not to ignoreA travel consultation does not end at the departure gate. An important part of the risk appears weeks after you arrive.The most important signal is simple to remember: fever after travel to a malaria-risk area is a medical emergency, even if you took prophylaxis correctly and even if you feel reasonably well between fever spikes. Malaria can deteriorate quickly, and early treatment changes the outcome.Fever, chills or sweats after travel to a risk area — seek medical assessment and always say where you have been.Diarrhoea that persists, contains blood, or comes with high fever.Jaundice — yellowing of the skin or eyes.A rash appearing after your return.An animal bite or scratch during the trip — this one is assessed immediately, while still travelling if possible.Always tell whoever sees you that you have travelled, where, and when. It is the information that changes the clinical reasoning, and the one most often left unsaid.When it is not time to book an appointmentChest pain, breathlessness at rest, sudden weakness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, confusion, neck stiffness with fever, or skin blotches that do not fade under pressure: call 112. If you are unsure how serious it is, contact SNS 24.PracticalWhat to settle before you leaveA short sequence that avoids almost every avoidable problem.Book the consultation as soon as the trip is decided, not the night before.Gather your vaccination record, your regular medication list and the itinerary with dates.Check whether the destination requires an international certificate of vaccination and, if so, book the CVI early.Carry regular medication in your hand luggage, in its original packaging, with the prescription or a medical letter.Check your health cover at the destination, including repatriation, and carry emergency contacts.Keep the consulate or embassy contact details, know how to seek medical help in the country you are visiting, and sign up to the Portuguese Ministry of Foreign Affairs traveller registry, which lets the consulate reach you in an emergency.None of this takes an afternoon. It only has to be done beforehand, rather than on the way to the airport — which is, in practice, when most people remember.Global Health PortugalNext stepsOur doctors in Portugal carry out the pre-travel assessment by video and tell you plainly what the consultation can settle and what requires attending an International Vaccination Centre.Travel health consultationOur doctors in PortugalContact Global Health PortugalTravelling and not sure where to start?A video consultation reviews your vaccination record, assesses the risk of your itinerary, prescribes what is indicated and tells you exactly what still has to be dealt with in person.Book a consultationSee our doctorsOfficial sourcesWhere to confirm the rulesLocations and hours, entry requirements by country and recommended vaccines all change.
