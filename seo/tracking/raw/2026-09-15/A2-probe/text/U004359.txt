Smoking cessation is an integral part of primary care. Your primary care physician assesses your level of dependence, designs a personalized plan, and — where clinically indicated after a full evaluation — may consider pharmacological support options. Structured medical support significantly multiplies the chances of success compared with trying without support.What pharmacological support options are available to quit smoking?There are various options for pharmacological support treatment for smoking cessation — including nicotine replacement therapy, bupropion, cytisine, and varenicline — with different profiles of efficacy, tolerability, and contraindications. The most appropriate option for you depends on your complete clinical profile. None can be confirmed or recommended before medical evaluation.Are these treatments funded by Social Security?Since June 2024, certain medications for smoking cessation are funded by the Ministry of Health for patients who meet specific clinical criteria — including smokers with high dependence. Funding through the public system requires a prescription from an SNS doctor. Your Global Health doctor will inform you during the consultation if you may meet the criteria and what steps to follow.How many consultations will I need?A structured smoking cessation plan generally includes an initial assessment and planning appointment, and follow-up appointments during the process — usually at 2 weeks, 1 month, and 3 months. Your doctor designs the appropriate follow-up schedule for your case.What happens if I have tried to quit smoking before and relapsed?Relapse is a normal part of the recovery process — it does not indicate failure, but rather that the plan needs to be adjusted. Previous unsuccessful attempts are clinically relevant information that your doctor uses to design a more effective approach. The more previous attempts there are, the more important structured medical support becomes.Can I use this service if I have health conditions such as diabetes or cardiovascular disease?Yes — and for these patients quitting smoking is especially a priority. Your doctor evaluates your complete clinical profile when designing the plan, including possible interactions with your current medication and the specific considerations of your health condition.Is this service suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding?If you are pregnant or breastfeeding and want to quit smoking, consult your doctor at the start of the appointment. The support options available during pregnancy are different — your doctor will advise you on the safest approach for your situation.Do I need Spanish health insurance?No. It is a private direct-payment service. The clinical documentation issued through this service is provided at the full private price regardless of insurance status.Can I use this service from anywhere in Spain?Yes — including the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla.
