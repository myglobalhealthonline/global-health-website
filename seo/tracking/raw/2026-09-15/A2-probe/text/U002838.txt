When your condition requires specialist dermatology assessment — including suspicious lesions, severe or treatment-resistant conditions, or conditions requiring patch testing, biopsy, or specialist procedures — your doctor issues a formal referral letter and advises on urgency.Skin Lesions — When to Act UrgentlyAny change in a mole or skin lesion warrants clinical assessment. The ABCDE criteria are the standard clinical guide:A — Asymmetry — one half of the lesion doesn't match the otherB — Border — irregular, notched, or poorly defined edgesC — Colour — variation in colour within the lesion — shades of brown, black, red, white, or blueD — Diameter — larger than 6mm — approximately the size of a pencil eraserE — Evolving — any change in size, shape, colour, or a new symptom such as bleeding or itchingIf your lesion meets any of these criteria, book an assessment promptly. Your doctor assesses the lesion visually and advises on whether urgent in-person dermatology assessment is needed.Skin cancer diagnosis requires in-person dermoscopy and, when indicated, biopsy. Dermatology is one of the medical specialties best suited to visual assessment, and a video consultation allows your doctor to directly observe the lesion, rash, or skin change. Good lighting and a clear camera image significantly improve the quality of the assessment. When a condition cannot be adequately assessed remotely — suspicious lesions requiring dermoscopy, conditions requiring biopsy or patch testing — your doctor advises clearly and coordinates the appropriate in-person referral.Can I get isotretinoin through this service?Yes, when clinically indicated for moderate to severe acne, with the appropriate monitoring protocol — including periodic tests and blood work. Your doctor explains what's required during the consultation.I have a mole that has changed — how urgent is this?Moles that have changed warrant prompt clinical assessment. Book a consultation and show the lesion to your doctor during the video call. Your doctor assesses it against standard clinical criteria and advises whether urgent in-person dermatology assessment is needed. If you're worried, don't wait — book a same-day consultation.Can I use this service for my child's eczema?Yes. Eczema in infants and children is one of the most common paediatric skin presentations and is a core part of this service. Your doctor assesses your child's eczema with you present throughout the consultation, and advises on flare-up management, emollient regimen, trigger avoidance, and ongoing care.Can I get a dermatology referral through this service?Yes. When specialist dermatology support is clinically indicated — including for suspicious lesions, severe or treatment-resistant conditions, or conditions requiring specialist procedures — your doctor issues a formal referral letter on the same day, addressed to the dermatologist or service of your choice.What if I've already been assessed by a dermatologist and need ongoing management?Bring any existing letters, reports, or treatment plans from your dermatologist to your consultation. Your doctor reviews them in full and manages your ongoing care at primary-care level, coordinating with your specialist as needed.Is this service suitable for fungal skin infections?Yes. Fungal skin infections — including ringworm, athlete's foot, pityriasis versicolor, and fungal scalp infections — are assessed and managed at primary-care level. Your doctor assesses the presentation and advises on appropriate treatment.What if my rash is spreading rapidly or I feel unwell?A rapidly spreading rash, accompanied by fever, difficulty breathing, facial swelling, or significant illness, is a medical emergency.
