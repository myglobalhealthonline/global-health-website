Hyperactivity often turns inward — restlessness, a mind that will not settle — while the inattentive side stays and gets expensive: missed deadlines, unopened admin, a working memory that keeps dropping things.What matters for a diagnosis is that the difficulties are long-standing, show up in more than one part of life, and cause real impairment. It is deliberately quick and deliberately over-inclusive, because its job is to decide who should be assessed properly — not to decide who has ADHD.It is not a diagnosis, and a positive result on its own means no more than "worth assessing".A negative result does not rule ADHD out, particularly for the inattentive presentation and particularly in women, who are diagnosed later and less often.Anxiety, depression, poor sleep, thyroid disease, chronic pain and heavy drinking all produce the same answers.It cannot separate ADHD from autism, from a specific learning difficulty, or from the effects of a long stretch of stress.It was designed and validated for adults. Children and teenagers need a different assessment entirely.It says nothing about treatment. This is a screener: it sorts people into "worth assessing properly" and "not on these answers". A diagnosis is made by a psychiatrist or a clinical psychologist, from a full interview about your history rather than from six questions.What is the ASRS?The Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale, developed with the World Health Organization. Part A is the six-question screener used here; the full scale has eighteen items. It is the most widely used adult ADHD screening instrument, which is why clinicians recognise the result you bring them.What counts as a positive result?Four or more of the six questions answered at or above that question's own threshold. The first three count from "Sometimes", the last three only from "Often" — the thresholds differ because the questions do, and a single flat cut-off would miss the inattentive presentation.Can I have ADHD if my result is negative?Yes. Six questions cannot cover a condition that presents differently at different ages and in different people, and the inattentive presentation in particular is under-detected — one reason women are diagnosed later and less often. If the difficulties are affecting your life, a negative screen is not a reason to stop asking.Who can diagnose ADHD?A psychiatrist or a clinical psychologist, depending on the country. A GP is normally where the process starts: they take the history, rule out the conditions that mimic ADHD — thyroid problems, sleep disorders, anxiety, depression — and refer you on for the assessment itself.Does this ADHD test store my answers?No. The scoring runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you select is sent to our servers or saved.How do I get assessed for ADHD in Ireland?Through a GP first. Adult ADHD is assessed by psychiatrists and clinical psychologists, whether that happens in the public mental health services or privately, and a GP referral is what starts either route. The GP also checks the things that can look like ADHD, such as thyroid function, sleep problems, anxiety and depression, before anyone assesses for it.Can I be prescribed ADHD medication from an online test?No, and be wary of anywhere that offers it. ADHD medicines are controlled drugs, started only after a full assessment and a diagnosis by a specialist, and prescribing afterwards is normally shared between that specialist and your GP. Our Irish-registered doctors can review a screening result, rule out the conditions that mimic ADHD and refer you on. They cannot diagnose it in a video call.Want to talk your result through?Book a video consultation with a registered doctor, go through your history and the conditions that can look like ADHD, and find out what an assessment would involve.Book a consultation
