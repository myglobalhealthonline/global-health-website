Below that it is hard to meet your protein, vitamin and mineral needs from food, and an intake that low should be medically supervised.02Choosing your activity levelThe activity multiplier is the biggest lever in this calculation: for the same body, the gap between sedentary and extremely active is more than 700 kcal a day. It covers everything you do, not just training: standing, walking, housework and your job all count.Most people pick one level too high. If you train three times a week and sit for the rest of it, you are lightly to moderately active, not very active.Sedentary, a desk job and little or no deliberate exercise.Lightly active, light exercise or sport one to three days a week.Moderately active, moderate exercise three to five days a week.Very active, hard exercise six or seven days a week.Extremely active, a physically demanding job, or hard training twice a day.03How the calculation worksYour basal metabolic rate (what the body burns at complete rest) comes from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: 10 × weight in kg, plus 6.25 × height in cm, minus 5 × age, then plus 5 for men or minus 161 for women.That figure is multiplied by your activity level, from 1.2 for sedentary up to 1.9 for extremely active. The result is your total daily energy expenditure, the maintenance number at the top of the read-out.Worked example: a 30-year-old man, 180 cm and 80 kg, has a basal rate of 1,780 kcal. Lightly active (× 1.375) that is about 2,448 kcal a day to maintain his weight.04What a calorie calculator cannot tell youEvery equation here is a population average fitted to measured data, and individuals sit either side of it. Treat the number as an estimate to test against the scales over three or four weeks, not as a fact about your body.Estimates are typically within about 10% for most adults. That is 200 kcal either way on a 2,000 kcal day.It says nothing about what those calories are made of, and protein, fibre and micronutrients decide whether a diet holds.Thyroid conditions, some medications and a long history of dieting all shift energy expenditure away from the estimate.It is not designed for children, for pregnancy or breastfeeding, or for athletes in heavy training blocks.Very muscular people are underestimated, because the equation uses total body weight rather than lean mass.Where to go from hereA calorie figure is a starting point, not a plan. For most adults that lands somewhere between 1,800 and 2,800 kcal, but the range is wide because it depends on your size, age and how active you are. The calculator above gives your own figure rather than the average.How many calories do I need to eat to lose weight?About 500 kcal a day below your maintenance figure gives roughly half a kilo a week, and 250 below gives about a quarter of a kilo. Bigger deficits do not reliably work faster. They are simply harder to keep, and more of the loss comes from muscle.Is 1,200 calories a day safe?It is the lowest intake this calculator will suggest, and it is a floor rather than a recommendation. Below roughly that level it becomes difficult to get enough protein, vitamins and minerals from food, so a lower intake should only be followed under medical supervision.Which formula does this calorie calculator use?Mifflin-St Jeor for the basal rate, then a standard activity multiplier. It replaced the older Harris-Benedict equation in most clinical use because it is more accurate for modern body compositions, typically to within about 10% for healthy adults.Do I have to count calories to lose weight?No. Counting is one method of holding a deficit, and plenty of people manage the same thing by changing what they eat rather than by weighing it. Knowing roughly where your maintenance level sits is useful either way, because it tells you how big a change you are actually making.Does this calorie calculator store my details?No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to our servers or saved.How many calories a day are recommended in Romania?Romanian food labels carry the EU reference intake of 2,000 kcal a day for an average adult, and national healthy-eating guidance uses figures of that order. They are population averages. Your own needs depend on height, weight, age and activity, which is what the calculator above estimates.Can a doctor in Romania review my calorie target?Yes.
