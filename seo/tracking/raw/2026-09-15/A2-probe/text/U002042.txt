The WHO suggests 23.0 and 27.5 as additional action points for those populations.02How BMI is calculatedBMI is your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres. That is the whole formula — there is no age, sex or body-composition term in it.Worked example: someone 1.75 m tall weighing 78 kg has a BMI of 78 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 25.5, which falls in the overweight band.Imperial entries are converted first (1 stone = 6.35 kg, 1 inch = 2.54 cm), so the result is identical either way.03What BMI does not tell youBMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis. It is useful precisely because it is crude — it needs only a tape measure and a scales — but that is also its limit.It cannot separate muscle from fat. Well-muscled people are routinely classed as overweight.It ignores where fat sits. Waist circumference predicts cardiometabolic risk better than BMI does.It is not validated for pregnancy — use your pre-pregnancy BMI for maternity care planning.Children and teenagers need age-and-sex centile charts, not the adult bands above.In adults over 65 a BMI slightly above 25 is not consistently linked to worse outcomes.04When to talk to a doctor about your weightYou have lost or gained weight without trying to.Your BMI is 30 or above, or 25 and above alongside high blood pressure, high cholesterol or raised blood sugar.You are being investigated for, or already treated for, type 2 diabetes, sleep apnoea, PCOS or fatty liver disease.You want to discuss medically supervised weight management rather than another diet.Where to go from hereBMI is a starting point, not a plan. Above 25 is classed as overweight and 30 or above as obesity. These are population thresholds, so a single reading is a prompt to look further, not a verdict.Is BMI calculated differently for men and women?No. The formula and the adult categories are identical. Women generally carry a higher proportion of body fat than men at the same BMI, which is one of the reasons the number is only a starting point.How accurate is a BMI calculator?The arithmetic is exact — the uncertainty is in what BMI measures. It correlates reasonably well with body fat across a population but can misclassify individuals, particularly athletes, older adults and people with a lot of muscle.What should I do if my BMI is in the obese range?Have your blood pressure, cholesterol and blood glucose checked, and talk to a doctor about a plan you can actually sustain. BMI is the first-line screening measure in Irish practice and is usually read alongside waist circumference, blood pressure and blood tests rather than on its own.
