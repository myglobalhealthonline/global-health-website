The same reading with dizziness, blurred vision, or fainting has causes that can be found.Low blood pressure, meaning under 90/60 mmHg, isn't a disease in itself. If you have no symptoms, the guidelines are clear that it doesn't need treatment. The problem appears when blood pressure drops by 20 mmHg or more on standing up and brings on dizziness, blacked-out vision, or fainting. In that case, the right response on the spot is to sit or lie down with your legs raised, drink water, and cross your legs while tensing your muscles. Sugar water, vitamins, and teas don't appear in any guideline. The NHS and NHLBI say the same thing: with no symptoms, it usually isn't treated. The second is orthostatic hypotension: the European guideline defines it as a drop in systolic pressure of at least 20 mmHg, or in diastolic pressure of 10 mmHg, within the first 3 minutes after standing up. This is what causes the dizziness on getting out of bed and the blackout in front of your eyes, and this is the one worth investigating.ESC 2018 guideline for syncope, NHS: low blood pressure, NHLBI. Accessed 7 September 2026.Where it comes fromCommon causes of low blood pressureMost causes are minor and reversible. A few aren't, which is why the list is worth going through.Low fluid intake: dehydration, heat, diarrhoea, vomiting. The most common cause in summer.Medications: diuretics, vasodilators, some antidepressants, heart and blood pressure medications, some sedatives. Check what you've started recently.After a meal: postprandial hypotension, especially in older adults and after large meals.Standing for long periods, prolonged bed rest, alcohol.Pregnancy, especially in the first two trimesters.Heart: heart failure, arrhythmias, heart attack.Nervous system: diabetes with nerve damage, Parkinson's disease.Addison's disease (adrenal insufficiency), where blood pressure drops mainly on changing position.Bleeding or severe infection: low blood pressure with a fast pulse, cold, clammy skin. An emergency.ESC 2018, causes table and MedlinePlus: hypotension.The European guidelineWhat to do when you have low blood pressure and feel dizzyThe steps below come from the ESC guideline and NHS recommendations. They work within minutes and cost nothing.Sit or lie down immediately, with your legs raised above heart level. If you can't, sit with your head between your knees.Counter-pressure manoeuvres: cross your legs and tense your thighs and buttocks, clench your fists, rise onto your toes. The guideline rates these class IIa, meaning recommended.Drink water. The guideline mentions rapid water intake; adequate hydration carries the same class of recommendation.Get up slowly, in stages: sit on the edge of the bed, then stand, with support.Long term, for confirmed orthostatic hypotension, the guideline recommends 2–3 litres of fluids a day, extra salt only on a doctor's advice, compression stockings, sleeping with the head of the bed raised, and cutting back on medications that lower blood pressure. Midodrine and fludrocortisone exist, but they're a specialist's decision, by prescription.ESC 2018, section 5.3 and NHS: fainting.Sugar water, vitamins, teasWhat actually raises low blood pressure, and what only seems toThe remedies most people look for don't appear in any European guideline. They aren't dangerous, but they don't do what people think either.Sugar water: the guidelines recommend water; sugar doesn't appear anywhere. Plain water does the same job.Coffee: a 2021 systematic review found only very small studies with inconsistent results, and recommends caffeine only after evidence-based treatments have been exhausted. It isn't a treatment.Vitamins: no vitamin appears in the guidelines as a treatment for low blood pressure. If the hypotension comes from anaemia or an endocrine condition, the underlying cause is treated, after testing.Teas, chocolate, extra salt: no evidence. The ESC guideline allows for increased salt intake only as a doctor-directed measure, in selected patients.When to call 112Fainting with chest pain, laboured breathing, a fast or irregular heartbeat, confusion, weakness on one side of the body, or slurred speech. Fainting during exercise or while lying down. Black stools, vomiting blood, high fever with low blood pressure, cold and clammy skin. The person doesn't fully recover within a minute. Calling 112 is free, from any network.Clinical Autonomic Research 2021, caffeine in orthostatic hypotension; Gov.ro: the 112 emergency service.Not an emergency, but worth an appointmentWhen to see a doctor for low blood pressureWhen the dizziness keeps recurring, when you've fainted once with no explanation, or when the low blood pressure appeared alongside a new medication.A family doctor or general practitioner can do the three things that matter: measuring blood pressure lying down and standing up, reviewing your medication list, and a basic set of tests, full blood count, blood glucose, thyroid function, electrolytes, plus an electrocardiogram. In an online doctor consultation with Global Health, you can go through your history and medication list and get referrals for tests. The standing measurement and the ECG still need to be done in person.Beforehand, measure for seven days, morning and evening, two readings a minute apart, seated, after five minutes of rest, with a validated upper-arm blood pressure monitor. A single low reading on a hot day doesn't mean anything; a low average, together with symptoms, does.Measurement protocol from the ESC 2024 guideline.Global Health RomâniaThe next stepYou can carry out the on-the-spot steps yourself.
