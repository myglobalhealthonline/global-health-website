Someone with a reading of 132/84 may hear ‘it is not that bad’ and assume it no longer matters. It does matter. It may not yet be confirmed hypertension, but it is not an ideal reading either and should be monitored if it recurs.Below 120/70: a clearly normal reading.Systolic 120-139 or diastolic 70-89: no longer the ideal range; repeat the measurement correctly and monitor the trend.Repeated readings of 140/90 or above: consistent with hypertension and requiring confirmation.A single reading does not establish a diagnosis on its own if it was measured incorrectly or under unfavourable circumstances.Patient information and materials from the Romanian Society of Cardiology: Cardioportal. The current European guideline remains the main clinical reference: ESC 2024.Where the misconception arisesWhat changes with age and what does notPeople search online for a separate table for ages 30, 50 or 70. Medicine looks for something else: how consistent the readings are, your overall risk and which other conditions are present.At 28, repeatedly high blood pressure matters because it has decades in which to cause harm. At 68, the same reading matters because it may occur alongside kidney disease, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, stroke or heart failure. It does not become ‘normal’ in either case.Young adult: hypertension may go unnoticed precisely because it rarely causes pain.Middle age: weight, smoking, cholesterol and blood glucose affect how closely it should be monitored.Older person: the treatment target may be individualised to avoid dizziness or falls, but this does not make a high reading acceptable.Pregnancy: this is a separate clinical situation and should not be combined with general adult tables.‘It is normal for your age’ is often a misleading statementWhat may differ for an older person is how treatment and monitoring are managed, not the definition of a reading that is too high.Two numbersWhat systolic and diastolic pressure meanSystolic pressure is the maximum pressure when the heart pumps blood. Diastolic pressure is the minimum pressure between heartbeats.The practical rule is simple: if either number is abnormal, the reading deserves attention. A reading of 152/78 is not reassuring simply because ‘the second number is good’. A reading of 126/94 is not good simply because the first number has not reached 14.High systolic pressure with a better diastolic reading: particularly common at older ages and should not be dismissed.High diastolic pressure: this also matters, especially when it recurs.Symptoms, pulse and test results help with interpretation, but they do not cancel out a series of high readings.The trend is more useful than a single number recorded on a bad morning.If you want to follow the trend rather than one isolated number, use the blood pressure calculator and record instead of treating every measurement as a final verdict.TechniqueHow to measure your blood pressure correctly at homeMany ‘bad readings’ are not truly bad; they were measured incorrectly. Correcting the technique is quicker than changing a treatment plan unnecessarily.Sit quietly for five minutes first, with your back supported and your legs uncrossed.Do not measure immediately after coffee, smoking, exercise or climbing stairs.Use an upper-arm monitor with the correct cuff size; wrist monitors are more prone to error.Keep your arm at heart level and do not talk during the measurement.Take two readings and record the time, circumstances and any symptoms.Repeat this over several days, not only after a stressful day or, conversely, an unusually calm one.A record like this makes the consultation useful. The doctor sees a pattern rather than a snapshot. Sound decisions can then be based on that pattern: simple monitoring, blood tests, an ECG, lifestyle changes or an adjustment to treatment.Cardioportal also provides patient guidance on correct home measurement: Cardioportal.The practical decisionWhen an appointment is enough and when to seek urgent careMost high readings are not an emergency. It becomes an emergency when a high reading occurs with symptoms suggesting acute harm.Book an appointment if you have high readings over several days, even if you feel well.Do not delay if you also have diabetes, kidney disease, are pregnant, have heart disease or have previously had a stroke.Seek urgent help if you develop chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness on one side, fainting, confusion or a sudden, severe headache.Do not take leftover ‘emergency’ medication from a previous episode without a plan agreed with your doctor.The number alone does not tell the whole storyA person with a reading of 180/110 and no symptoms does not have the same priority as someone with 170/100 plus chest pain. Symptoms change everything.What a consultation can doWhat an online chronic disease consultation can addressIt offers more than a ‘prescription renewal’: it can turn a disorganised collection of readings into a clear follow-up plan.For stable hypertension, a GP can coordinate monitoring, current treatment and the management of risk factors. During an online chronic disease consultation, your home readings, symptoms, prescribed medication, test results and cardiovascular risk can all be reviewed.If readings remain uncontrolled, symptoms develop, organ damage is suspected or specialist tests are needed, the GP may recommend cardiology or an in-person assessment. Having an organised record already available makes this transition clearer and faster.Interpretation of a properly completed home blood pressure record.Review of previously prescribed antihypertensive treatment and how well it is tolerated.Assessment of blood pressure alongside other chronic conditions: diabetes, kidney disease and dyslipidaemia.Clear guidance on when cardiology or urgent assessment is needed.That is the difference between an article that merely attracts traffic and one that can support a genuine clinical next step.Global Health RomaniaNext stepsIf you already have several readings outside the normal range, another search is not the useful next step. Urgency is determined by the combination of the reading and symptoms.Can an online consultation genuinely help me?Yes, particularly for interpreting repeated readings, reviewing treatment and deciding whether tests or cardiology input are needed.
