Global Health SEO roadmap review

Reviewed August 25, 2026

Organic visibility is moving in the right direction, but the next-phase execution drifted away from the evidence-backed plan. The main gap is not content volume. It is over-production of locale variants before measurement, weak lead attribution, and no real off-site authority program. This review covers the current roadmap, the Week 2 draft pipeline, live Search Console and OpenSEO evidence, and the changes most likely to improve lead quality rather than just URL count.

Where the site stands

The site is not in a technical emergency. Search Console page data for July 26, 2026 through August 22, 2026 shows blog traffic is no longer hypothetical: the Ireland sick certificate post, the Ireland Illness Benefit post, the Czech eNeschopenka post, the Portugal self-declaration post, and the Romania medical-letter post are all earning impressions and clicks.

The roadmap problem is execution drift. The editorial plan explicitly rejected blanket six-locale fan-out, but Week 2 was prepared as 36 standalone files and none of them were actually created in the production CMS. At the same time, the canonical SEO ledger says the six-market program is in monitor mode with dated September checkpoints, not a fresh high-volume publishing sprint.

If you only do one thing

Replace the volume-first multilingual content sprint with a measurement-first, selective-locale publishing program.

That change addresses the biggest current risks at once: cannibalization, clinician-review bottlenecks, low-value translation volume, and publishing too many pages before the new GA4 funnel data can prove whether blog traffic creates bookings.

  1. Freeze the six-locale Week 2 seeding plan and reduce it to a small cohort: native-market version first, plus English or one proven migrant-corridor variant only where the Week 1 pattern already justifies it.
  2. Use next week for three tracks in parallel: publish a small reviewed cohort, fix the content pipeline rules, and start real authority outreach to partner and health-relevant domains.
  3. Check success with the September 1, September 8, and September 30 milestones already registered in the canonical SEO ledger, then expand only the topics and locales that earn search demand and lead signals.

Small fixes, in order

Week 2 drifted away from the plan high

The evidence-backed Week 2 matrix contains exactly 19 locale variants, with locale fan-out only where market or migration-corridor evidence exists. The working set drifted to 36 standalone articles across all six topics and six languages.

Fix: revert Week 2 to the 19-variant rule. Prepare the six primary-market articles first, then add only the 13 approved translations after native-language review.

Week 2 is not in the CMS high

The corrected Week 2 delivery record now states the production state consistently: 36 standalone copies exist locally, but no Week 2 CMS records were created. A read-only production audit confirmed that the batch is local only.

Fix: stop treating Week 2 as "drafted and loaded." It is only researched and prepared locally. Re-approve the smaller cohort first, then create those CMS drafts deliberately.

The canonical roadmap is in monitor mode high

The SEO ledger states that no further SEO implementation is currently justified and that there is no Wave 4 in the current six-market strategy. The scheduled work is a dated September measurement calendar: September 1, September 8, September 30, and November 13, 2026, plus a few event-driven checks.

Fix: separate two workstreams. Keep the canonical SEO monitor calendar intact, and run the blog program as a tightly-scoped growth experiment that respects the same measurement gates instead of overriding them.

Lead attribution is not ready yet high

The search-opportunity join for July 26 to August 22, 2026 found 717 candidate Search Console rows, but only 9 matched GA4 landing rows and every matched row showed 0 key events and 0 transactions. That period ends before the August 25, 2026 conversion instrumentation verification, so it cannot answer blog ROI yet.

Fix: do not judge blogs by leads yet. Use clicks, impressions, rank movement, CTA placement, and assisted-path checks for now, then re-measure booking and purchase signals after post-August 25 data accumulates.

The latest audit is not a sitewide content verdict medium

The focused OpenSEO audit completed 100 of 100 pages and found 1 thin-content warning, 93 long meta descriptions, and 68 long titles. But the crawled pages were essentially the homepage plus Ireland service translations. The audit returned 0 blog pages, 0 tools pages, and 0 doctor pages.

Fix: treat those counts as a narrow metadata sample, not a reason to rewrite the whole site. The only immediate content-quality flag is the thin homepage warning. The rest should be folded into future template-tightening, not emergency SEO work.

Authority remains the ceiling medium

The 25 August OpenSEO snapshot shows rank 43, 551 backlinks, 391 referring pages, and 62 referring domains. The Week 2 research record holds the prior day's 550 / 390 / 62 snapshot, so the one-link difference is normal provider movement rather than a conflicting baseline. The profile remains dominated by `wix.to` and a May to July directory/article burst. The Week 2 plan includes prospects, but no outreach has been sent and no new acquired links are recorded.

Fix: move one real off-site action into every weekly cycle: partner-link requests, regulator/profile completions, or editorial outreach on the highest-converting country-specific topics. Publishing without authority work will keep capping competitive gains.

New content should be shorter and narrower medium

Many service and draft pages are 1,100 to 2,600 words. That was defensible for the earlier evidence-heavy buildout, but it does not match the new operating rule you set: shorter, faster, rank-focused pages. It also increases clinician-review load, which the original plan already called the binding constraint.

Fix: set a new default brief: 600 to 900 words for administrative/process topics, 700 to 1,200 for clinical safety topics, one intent, one CTA family, 2 to 4 FAQs only when they answer real query variants, and no filler sections added just to reach length.

Where to focus first

Focus on short, service-adjacent, country-specific topics that the current site already proves it can rank for or that no current page answers cleanly. Avoid publishing more generic blood-pressure, ADHD, or lab explainers where a tool, service page, or existing article already covers the intent and the canonical roadmap is waiting for measurement.

Ireland admin/process existing demand

The sick-certificate and Illness Benefit pair already carry the strongest blog evidence on the site. The winning pattern is local process, rights, eligibility, rates, and employer workflow.

Make: keep Ireland content short, exact, and procedural. Prioritize amount, payment timing, and employer-vs-state distinctions over broad health explainers.

Portugal admin/service bridge 4400 to 1900 searches/mo

`baixa medica` amount and driving-certificate eligibility are both local, recurring, and directly connected to live services.

Make: a small Portugal cohort first: `baixa medica` amount/calculation and driving-certificate group rules, then expand only if the first cohort earns traction and CTA activity.

Czech sick-pay follow-up 1600 searches/mo

The Czech process article already ranks. The missing companion is the money-calculation angle, not another generic health explainer.

Make: a concise Czech calculation article that clearly splits employer days from CSSZ payment days and links to the existing process guide.

Romania myth-correction safety 9900 searches/mo

The Romanian rapid blood-pressure query cluster is large, but the safe angle is triage and myth correction, not remedies or dosing.

Make: one short Romanian safety page that answers what to do after a high reading, when not to self-medicate, and when to seek urgent care, tied to chronic-disease consultation.

Already working

  • Administrative, country-specific blog topics already outperform generic medical translations. The site has proof of product-market fit in this content class.
  • Migration-corridor translations can work when they explain a local process in the worker's language. Ireland Romanian and Czech German examples already rank.
  • The technical foundation is mostly stable. The current bottleneck is prioritization, not a broad crawl-health failure.
  • Localized review copy, native insurance text, the single doctors-directory heading, and the gated llms.txt links are live. The canonical ledger now closes only the items verified in production and leaves the consented widget and anchor scan as explicit follow-ups.
  • The service and tool footprint gives strong internal-link targets. Short blog pages can still be useful because the commercial path already exists elsewhere on the site.
