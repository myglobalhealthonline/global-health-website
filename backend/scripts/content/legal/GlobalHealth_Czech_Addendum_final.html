CZ-2. Medical Regulation in the Czech Republic

Medical practice in the Czech Republic is regulated by the Česká lékařská komora (ČLČ) under Zákon č. 220/1991 Sb. All Clinicians providing consultations to patients in the Czech Republic through the Platform hold current registration with the ČLČ.

Each Clinician's ČLČ registration number is displayed on their individual profile page on the Platform. Patients may verify a Clinician's registration via the ČLČ online register at www.lkcr.cz.

Clinicians registered in other EU member states who provide services to Czech patients do so under the mutual recognition provisions of EU Directive 2005/36/EC on the recognition of professional qualifications, subject to any applicable notification requirements under Czech law.

All Clinicians practising under the Global Health IČPE registration comply with the ethical and professional standards of the ČLČ and with the clinical governance framework of Global Health, including applicable guidance on telemedicine and remote clinical practice issued by the Ministerstvo zdravotnictví ČR (MZČR).

Global Health maintains a clinical governance framework that includes: ongoing verification of Clinician ČLČ registration status; compliance monitoring; clinical audit; and a formal complaints and clinical incident review procedure in accordance with Zákon č. 372/2011 Sb.

CZ-3. Prescription Services in the Czech Republic

CZ-3.1 Electronic prescriptions

Prescriptions in the Czech Republic are issued electronically through the SÚKL eRecept system, in compliance with Zákon č. 378/2007 Sb. (Pharmacy Act, as amended) and applicable SÚKL regulations. Prescriptions are issued under Global Health's eRecept certificate (AMBSUKL216453413G) by individually credentialled Clinicians holding their own SÚKL zdravotník registration.

Following a consultation, the electronic prescription reference is transmitted to the patient via the eRecept system. Patients may collect their medication at any participating pharmacy in the Czech Republic using their national identification number (rodné číslo) or prescription identifier.

CZ-3.2 Prescription validity

Electronic prescriptions issued through the Platform are valid for dispensing at pharmacies across the Czech Republic in accordance with SÚKL regulations. Prescription validity periods are determined by the prescribing Clinician in accordance with applicable clinical and regulatory guidelines.

CZ-3.3 Controlled substances

Prescriptions for narcotic and psychotropic substances (omamné a psychotropní látky) in Lists I and II under Zákon č. 167/1998 Sb. (as amended), which require special prescription formalities, are not issued through the Platform. Prescriptions for substances in Lists III and IV, including benzodiazepines and other medications routinely prescribed in general practice, may be issued where clinically appropriate following a full consultation and in accordance with the prescribing Clinician's professional judgment and applicable ČLČ guidance.

CZ-3.4 Cross-border prescription validity

Prescriptions issued through the eRecept system are valid in the Czech Republic. Cross-border recognition within the EU is subject to EU Directive 2011/24/EU on patients' rights in cross-border healthcare and the national implementation of that Directive in the receiving country. Patients intending to use a Czech prescription outside the Czech Republic should verify its acceptance in advance. Global Health does not guarantee acceptance of Czech prescriptions by pharmacies or health systems outside the Czech Republic.

CZ-4.2 Patient rights under the Act on Health Services

As a patient receiving healthcare services in the Czech Republic, you have specific rights under Zákon č. 372/2011 Sb., including the right to: be informed about your health condition and proposed treatment; give or withhold consent to treatment; access your medical records; and have your dignity and privacy respected during the provision of healthcare services.

CZ-4.3 Statutory 14-day cooling-off period

For services booked online (distance contracts), you have a statutory 14-day right of withdrawal from the date of entering the contract, under the Občanský zákoník (§1829 et seq.). You are not required to give any reason for withdrawal within this period.

If you expressly request that a consultation take place within the 14-day cooling-off period and the consultation is completed, you waive your right to cancel that specific consultation. By booking a consultation to occur within 14 days of the contract date, you expressly acknowledge and consent to this waiver.

This cooling-off right applies to individual consultation bookings and to Credit Plan subscriptions. For Credit Plans, the 14-day cooling-off period runs from the date of first subscription. If no Credits have been used during the cooling-off period, you are entitled to a full refund. If Credits have been used, a proportionate deduction reflecting the value of Credits used will apply.

CZ-4.4 Credit Plan — no minimum term

Credit Plans available in the Czech Republic operate on a monthly rolling basis with no minimum contract term and no cancellation fee. You may cancel at any time in accordance with Clause 7.5 of the Master Terms. Your statutory rights under Czech law apply in addition to these contractual rights.

CZ-4.5 Refunds

Refunds to which you are entitled under the Master Terms, this Addendum, or Czech consumer law will be processed to your original payment method within 14 days of the refund decision being confirmed.

CZ-5. Data Protection in the Czech Republic

CZ-5.1 Applicable law and supervisory authority

Data processing for patients in the Czech Republic is governed by GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and Zákon č. 110/2019 Sb. (Act on Personal Data Processing), which implements GDPR in the Czech Republic.

As Global Guest s.r.o. is incorporated in the Czech Republic, the Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů (ÚÔÚ) is the lead supervisory authority for Global Health under GDPR's one-stop-shop mechanism. The sole obligation is to notify the DPO's contact details under Article 37(7) GDPR.

You have the right to lodge a complaint with the ÚÔú at any time: Pplk. Sochora 27, 170 00 Praha 7. Website: www.uoou.cz.

CZ-5.3 Legal bases for processing Health Data

As a registered healthcare provider in the Czech Republic, Global Health processes patient Health Data as a data controller on the following legal bases:

  • Article 9(2)(a) GDPR — your explicit consent, obtained at registration and reaffirmed before each consultation.
  • Article 9(2)(h) GDPR — processing necessary for the purposes of preventive medicine, medical diagnosis, the provision of health or social care or treatment, carried out by a health professional subject to professional secrecy obligations under Czech law.
  • Zákon č. 372/2011 Sb. — processing of patient health data by a registered healthcare provider as required for the delivery of health services.

CZ-5.4 Clinical records retention

Clinical records for patients in the Czech Republic are retained for a minimum of 10 years from the date of the last consultation, in accordance with Vyhláška č. 98/2012 Sb. (Decree on Medical Documentation) and applicable MZČR guidance.

Records relating to patients who were under 18 at the time of treatment are retained until the patient's 28th birthday or for 10 years from the last date of treatment, whichever is the longer.

CZ-5.5 Financial records retention

Financial and billing records are retained for 10 years in accordance with Czech tax legislation (Zákon č. 563/1991 Sb., Accounting Act, and Zákon č. 235/2004 Sb., VAT Act).

CZ-5.6 Language of processing notices

Data processing notices, consent requests, and privacy communications are provided to patients in both English and Czech. Where any discrepancy exists between the English and Czech versions of such notices, the English version prevails.

CZ-7. Liability — Czech Republic (Provider Model)

As Global Health operates in the Czech Republic under the Provider Model as a registered healthcare provider (IČPE: 11116233), Global Health accepts institutional responsibility for the clinical governance framework under which Services are delivered. This means:

  • Global Health is responsible for verifying and maintaining the ČLČ registration status of all Clinicians delivering services to Czech patients through the Platform.
  • Global Health maintains professional indemnity cover appropriate to its role as the registered healthcare provider in the Czech Republic, in accordance with Zákon č. 372/2011 Sb.
  • Individual Clinicians also maintain their own professional indemnity insurance as required by their ČLČ registration obligations.
  • Liability for individual clinical decisions rests with the treating Clinician, who exercises independent professional judgment. Global Health's institutional liability relates to governance, systems, and oversight.

Nothing in this clause limits or excludes any liability that cannot be excluded under Czech law or EU consumer law, including liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence.

Patient rights under Zákon č. 372/2011 Sb. (Act on Health Services) are not affected by the limitation of liability provisions in Clause 11 of the Master Terms.
